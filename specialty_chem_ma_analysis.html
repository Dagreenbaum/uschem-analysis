Implied Multiple Range Logic

Score 5–8 dimensions as "High" → 10–14x range. Mix of High/Mid → 8–11x. Majority Low or Mid → 5–8x. A single "Low" on Tech/Digital or End Market Quality anchors the range down even if others score well.

PE vs. Strategic Buyer Impact

Strategic buyers in 2025 paid 10.3x median (RL Hulett) vs. PE at 12.3x median. Counterintuitively, PE paid more — reflecting their buy-and-build thesis and dry powder. Best outcome for USCHEM: competitive tension between both buyer types.

The "Tech" Premium Question

The name implies a software or tech-enabled moat. If the business genuinely has proprietary process software or AI-driven formulation, it could command 2–4 additional turns vs. a pure chemical company — potentially putting it in the 12–15x band regardless of EBITDA size.

Read-Through for USCHEM

The comps consistently show 10–12x for quality specialty chemistry assets sold to strategics, and 9–13x for institutional PE platform builds. The Dipsol deal (10.5x by KWR) is the single best precedent — same buyer type (industrial chem strategic), similar size tier, similar product category.

Buyer Competition Matters

If both KWR and SK Capital are bidding on USCHEM, PE's buy-and-build premium creates tension. In 2025, PE paid a higher median (12.3x) than strategics (10.3x) — counterintuitive but driven by PE's multiple arbitrage thesis and dry powder deployment pressure.

USCHEM's Tech Premium Wildcard

If "Chemical Technologies" reflects genuine software or AI-enabled chemistry differentiation, ESI's ~18x NTM forward multiple and Balchem's ~19–20x are the relevant ceiling comps — not the 10–12x industrial process chemical benchmarks. That is a 5–8 turn upside to the multiple conversation.
